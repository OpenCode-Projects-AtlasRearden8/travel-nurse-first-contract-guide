# Chapter 4 — Comparing Pay Packages Honestly

Three recruiters send you three spreadsheets. One says $2,900/week. One says $2,300/week. One says "up to $3,400." Which one pays best? The honest answer: *none of them, as printed* — because they are measuring different things. Here is how to turn them into numbers you can actually compare.

## The trick: separate the buckets

A travel paycheck has two very different kinds of money:

1. **Taxable hourly wage** — the number your paycheck, overtime calculations, and future benefit records are built on.
2. **Nontaxable allowances** — housing stipends, meals & incidentals (M&I), and similar. These are *not* simply extra salary; their tax treatment has real rules, which is exactly why a tax pro belongs in your corner before this gets complicated. *(Education only — not tax advice.)*

Different spreadsheets shout different numbers because some quote the *sum* of both buckets and others only the taxable portion. Your job is to rebuild each offer with the buckets separated.

## The comparison worksheet

For each offer, write down:

| Row | What to record | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Taxable hourly rate | The floor — and what OT is calculated from |
| 2 | Guaranteed hours/week | Multiplied by row 1 = guaranteed weekly base |
| 3 | Housing stipend per week (and number of weeks) | Add/subtract depending on your housing plan |
| 4 | M&I or other allowances per week | Same |
| 5 | Overtime, holiday, on-call rates | The realistic upside |
| 6 | Estimated total per week (working weeks) | Rows 2 + 3 + 4 |
| 7 | Assignment length × row 6 | Your real contract total |
| 8 | Any out-of-pocket costs you front (licenses, deposits, scrubs) that are not reimbursed | Money out |

Then — and this part matters — adjust for your actual life:

- **Do you take the agency's housing or your own?** If provided housing, subtract the stipend-equivalent value when comparing against stipend-plus-self-housing offers. Compare *what you keep*, not what is promised.
- **Add one-off costs:** deposits, pet fees, travel to the assignment, a rental car if the city demands one.
- **Note worker-side premiums you care about:** night shifts vs days, ratio-heavy ICUs, holiday expectations.

## The honest-comparison questions

Ask each recruiter the same five things so your rows are comparable:

1. "What is the **taxable hourly rate**, and are the guaranteed hours per week written in the contract?"
2. "What stipends/allowances are in the package, per week, and for how many weeks of a 13-week contract?"
3. "If the hospital cancels a shift, is the guaranteed-hours pay still paid?"
4. "What are the cancellation terms *and* the completion bonus, in writing?"
5. "What reimbursements (licensing, travel, parking/scrubs/certs) are included, and are there caps?"

Two offers rarely differ by as much as their headlines do once these are answered. Often the "smaller" offer wins on guaranteed hours or cancellation terms — that is exactly what the worksheet exists to reveal.

## What "too good to be true" looks like

- Headline weekly numbers that collapse the moment you ask whether the *housing stipend is included in them.*
- "Up to $X" numbers that only happen with overtime shifts the unit has not promised.
- Guarantees that exist on the phone but not in the written contract.
- Offers significantly above regional norms with a *first-week cancellation clause* you discovered only in week four. (Well — that one is really "too good to read.")

## Real-life arithmetic example (dummy numbers)

Two identical positions, 13 weeks:

- **Offer A:** $34/hr taxable × 40 = $1,360/week base + $300/week housing + $120/week M&I = **$1,780/week**
- **Offer B:** $28/hr taxable × 40 = $1,120/week + $450/week housing + $150/week M&I = **$1,720/week**

Offer A prints "lower," and yet A's higher taxable wage means: (a) larger overtime potential; (b) a bigger base if hospital cancellations eat a stipend week; (c) state-to-state differences in *how* stipends are treated. Numbers like these are why the worksheet exists rather than the headline graphic. *(All figures here are invented for illustration.)*

## Chapter 4 takeaways

- Separate **taxable pay** from **allowances** in every package before comparing.
- Compare **what you keep** for your actual housing plan, not the brochure number.
- Ask all recruiters the same five questions; otherwise you are comparing apples to marketing.
- If a guarantee, cancellation term, or bonus is not in writing — treat it as not existing.
