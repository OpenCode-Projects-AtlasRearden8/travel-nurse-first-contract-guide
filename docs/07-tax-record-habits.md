# Chapter 7 — Tax-Record Habits

*Education only. This chapter is about habits for keeping records — not tax advice. Tax rules for travelers are famously fact-specific (domicile, duplicate housing, per-diem treatment, state filing). Before you make real tax decisions, talk to a tax professional who actually works with travel nurses. The goal of this chapter is simple: when that meeting happens, you arrive with tidy records instead of a shoebox.*

## Why records matter more for travelers than staff nurses

A traveler's tax life has moving parts a standard job doesn't:

- A **home state** and one or more **work states**, each possibly with filing obligations.
- **Allowances** (housing stipends, meals & incidentals) that receive special tax treatment under specific conditions.
- **Reimbursements** for licenses, travel, scrubs — some of which have documentation rules.
- Multiple **employers or agencies** in one year, each issuing its own wage statement.

None of this is scary. It is just paperwork that rewards a system — and a system is ten minutes a week, not a crisis in April.

## The 10-minute weekly habit

Once a week (Friday before clock-out is memorable), spend ten minutes:

1. **Snap your pay stub.** Screenshot or photo, saved into a folder for that contract.
2. **Check the math out loud.** Stated hourly rate, stipend lines, the week's actual hours. If a number is off, message your recruiter *that day* — pay errors are trivial to fix in week two and stubborn in month three.
3. **Log expenses into one place.** One note or one spreadsheet, dated: license renewals, parking, tolls, scrubs, union dues if any, CDC-relevant receipts, whatever your tax preparer later tells you matters.
4. **Save housing documents.** Lease renewals, rent receipts, utility bills — the documents that speak to where you actually live.

That is it. Four items, ten minutes, and April-you will owe you a coffee.

## What to keep (and where)

A practical folder structure (paper or digital):

```
tax-records/
├── 2026/
│   ├── paystubs/
│   │   ├── contract-1-austin/...
│   │   └── contract-2-denver/...
│   ├── housing/
│   │   ├── leases/
│   │   └── rent-receipts/
│   ├── licensing/
│   │   └── renewals-receipts/
│   ├── reimbursements/
│   └── mileage-log.md
└── 2025/...
```

Digital is fine — most people's phones photograph a pay stub perfectly well. The point is **one folder per year**, so nobody's April involves archaeology.

## The documents that matter most

- **Every wage statement from every agency** — keep them until they are reconciled against your year-end tax documents.
- **Assignment contracts** for the year — your tax preparer may want to see the structure, not just the numbers.
- **Proof of residence** for your home state: lease or mortgage statement, voter registration, vehicle registration, a couple of utility bills. These speak to where you genuinely live, which is the heart of several traveler-specific rules.
- **Licenses and renewals**, with receipts.
- **Any reimbursement letters** from your agency explaining what a payment covers.

## Questions to bring to a tax professional

Bring this list to your meeting. Not to quiz them — to make the meeting efficient:

1. "Given my specific situation, what records do you want me to keep, and in what format?"
2. "Which of my allowances and reimbursements have special treatment, and what documentation do *you* need for them?"
3. "Which states will I be required to file in this year, based on my assignments?"
4. "What should I stop doing in how I keep records?"
5. "When should I check back in with you — at year-end, or before I sign the next contract?"

## Three habits worth adopting from day one

1. **Separate accounts.** One checking account per contract (or per agency at minimum). Pay in, stipends in, expenses out — clean trails beat mental math.
2. **Photograph receipts immediately.** The receipt in your scrub pocket is gone by Tuesday; the photo lives forever.
3. **Keep tax stuff out of general email.** A dedicated folder or a notes app with a "tax" tag — so the license renewal email is findable in 11 months without a treasure hunt.

## What *not* to do

- Do not hold cash three jobs deep in one account and guess about stipends.
- Do not rely on a recruiter for tax interpretations. Recruiters are generally not tax professionals, and neither is this guide — that is what the professional is for.
- Do not delete agency emails about reimbursements or housing. Storage costs nothing; reconstruction costs weekends.

## Chapter 7 takeaways

- Weekly ten-minute record habit beats an April emergency, every time.
- One folder per year, four items per week: pay stub, math-check, expense log, housing docs.
- Your **home state's** records (lease, registration, tax filings) are the backbone documents.
- Reimbursements deserve receipts, and recruiters deserve questions but professionals deserve the judgment calls.
