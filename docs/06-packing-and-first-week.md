# Chapter 6 — Packing and the First-Week Routine

The nervous part of travel nursing is not the contract. It is the first 96 hours in a new city — arrive tired, work not-yet-familiar hallways, cook dinner in a kitchen with one pan. This chapter is designed to make those 96 hours boring, which is the highest compliment this guide can give them.

## The packing philosophy

Travel light where the housing unit is decent and *strategic* where it is not. The full copy-me checklist lives in **Appendix B**; the philosophy in five lines:

1. **Two-of-the-consumable rule.** Pack two weeks of anything: scrubs, socks, toothpaste. Reorder from the new city once you know it.
2. **Do not pack what a hospital provides.** Most facilities supply basics; your energy budget matters more than your storage budget.
3. **The license packet goes on your person** — never in a checked bag or a car trunk without photo backup.
4. **Keep a "first-night box"** in the car cabin: sheets, towel, phone charger, toiletries, pillow, instant coffee, snacks. Arriving at 8 p.m. should never require unloading a whole car.
5. **Comfort has a small budget.** A good pillow and a familiar candle are allowed. You are not a robot.

## The documents pouch

Keep these on your person, in a single folder or clear sleeve:

- Driver's license or state ID
- Nursing license (physical card if you have one) + a **photo or pdf copy**
- CPR/BLS card
- Immunization records (hepatitis B, MMR/flu titers as requested)
- Health requirements: TB results, physical/vaccination records
- Health insurance card; a list of your medications
- Emergency contact info (written, not just in your phone)
- Offer letter, assignment confirmation, recruiter's phone number
- Voided check or direct deposit details (required first week)
- A pen. (You'll want it, and their breakroom will not have one.)

## The first-week routine (day by day)

### Day 0 — arrive, inspect, sleep

Run the full **housing inspection** from Chapter 5 while you have fresh eyes. Photograph rooms. Make the bed. Lay out tomorrow's uniform and your lunch. Sleep.

### Day 1 — the new city, without the hospital

Don't spend this day at orientation-brain. Grocery shop for the week, locate the gas station, drive the hospital route **at the actual time you will be driving it for work**, and figure out parking. Optional hero move: drive past the *parking garage entrance*, not just the building.

### Day 2 or 3 — orientation day

Arrive early. Bring documents, ID badge paperwork, and patience. Collect: your unit's phone number, where the break room is, what the badge reader *doesn't* open. Eat the lunch you packed.

### Days 3–5 — build your repeat habits

- **A wake-up time you can sustain** for a full night-shift or day-shift rotation.
- **A meal plan you personally cooked once** — you will fall back on it around week six.
- **One load of laundry as a scheduled event**, not a chaos event.
- **Save the local numbers** (urgent care, the maintenance contact, the hospital employee health line) in your phone under a "new city" contacts group.

### End of week one

Do a ten-minute review in your notes document:

- What worked? What hurt?
- Any pay or schedule items that need a chat with the recruiter?
- What do you want to see in this city *besides* the hospital — schedule one of those things now.

## Small habits that pay off all thirteen weeks

- **Home base:** designate one drawer/desk spot for keys, badge, and wallet. In a new home, misplacing things is the default state.
- **Mid-assignment hardware check:** batteries, filters, the smoke detector again. Still working at week 7.
- **The last-week plan:** set a calendar reminder three weeks before the assignment ends for housing move-out tasks, deposit photos, and the extension-or-next-contract decision.

## Chapter 6 takeaways

- The packing philosophy is *first-night box + documents on your person + everything else lite*.
- First-week success is a **routine** question, not a hustle question — make the boring stuff easy.
- The housing inspection, done again in a new unit, is the single highest-value arrival ritual.
- Write down what worked each week. Future-you, in city #4, inherits a manual that actually works.
