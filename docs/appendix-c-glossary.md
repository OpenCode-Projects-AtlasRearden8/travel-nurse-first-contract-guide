# Appendix C — Glossary

*Travel nursing has its own dialect. Here is the translator. Definitions are educational and simplified; where a term touches law, licensing, or taxes, the official source (your board, the IRS, the agency's contract) is the final authority.*

## A

**Agency / staffing agency** — The company that employs you on a temporary contract and places you at a hospital. In W2 setups, the agency is your actual employer.

**Assignment** — One contracted tour at one facility, typically 8, 13, or 26 weeks.

**Allowance** — Money paid for a specific purpose (housing, meals) rather than as direct wages. See *stipend*.

## B

**Bill rate** — The total amount the hospital pays (to the agency, not to you) for your hours. Travelers generally don't need this number, but knowing it exists explains agency margins.

**Boutique agency** — A smaller, often regional staffing company.

## C

**Cancellation** — A contract or shift that ends before its scheduled finish — initiated by the agency, by the hospital, or (rarely, at a cost) by you. Read Chapter 2 before signing anything.

**Clause** — One provision inside the contract. The ones to know cold: guaranteed hours, cancellations, termination, float, extension.

**Compact / NLC (Nurse Licensure Compact)** — The agreement among participating states that lets a nurse with a **multistate license** practice in other member states, based on their **primary state of residence**. Verify details at nursecompact.com and your state board's site.

**Credentialing** — The documentation slog: licenses, immunizations, background checks, unit-specific training. It sets your real start date.

## D

**Differential (shift diff.)** — Extra hourly pay for night shifts, weekend shifts, charge duties, etc.

## E

**Employer of record** — The company legally employing you and responsible for payroll taxes. Ask who it is; it is not always the agency whose logo is on the job posting.

**Extension** — Continuing an assignment beyond its original end date. Terms can change between the original contract and the extension — recheck everything.

## F

**Filed/float** — Being assigned (sometimes mid-shift) to a different unit than your home unit. A float clause should name which units and required competencies.

## G

**Guaranteed hours (guarantee)** — The written promise that you will be paid a set number of weekly hours regardless of low census — *if* you meet the contract's own scheduling rules. Clouded by call-out penalties; read the fine print.

## H

**Housing stipend** — A periodic (usually weekly) allowance intended to cover your lodging while on assignment. Distinct from agency-provided housing. Its tax treatment is fact-specific — that is professional-advice territory.

## L

**License verification (Nursys)** — The national system many boards use to verify license details to another state.

**Local travel contract** — A contract near your home; "local" is defined by the agency (often a mileage radius), and its definition affects stipends.

## M

**M&I (meals & incidentals)** — An allowance, alongside housing, intended for daily living costs while traveling.

**MSP (Managed Service Provider)** — A company that manages *all* traveler staffing for a hospital system and runs the bidding between their enrolled agencies.

**Multistate license** — A license valid in all NLC member states, issued by your compact home state when you meet its rules.

## O

**OT (overtime)** — In a typical W2 travel setup, pay above 40 hours per week at 1.5× the hourly rate — but confirm the specific contract's language and your state's overtime rules.

## P

**Pay package** — The bundle: taxable hourly rate, stipends, differentials, bonus terms, reimbursements — everything your income is made of.

**Per diem** — A daily allowance for expenses; related to but different from a weekly housing stipend.

**Primary state of residence (PSOR)** — The state where you genuinely live, vote, register vehicles, and file taxes — the state whose compact license (if any) you can hold. Not the state of the agency's office.

## R

**Recruiter** — Your agency-side point of contact: the person sourcing jobs, negotiating pay, and (usually) wrangling your paperwork. Worth interviewing on merit.

## S

**Shift diff.** — See *differential*.

**Stipend** — See *housing stipend*; a generic term for purpose-tagged money. Tax treatment is fact-specific — a professional's territory.

## T

**Taxable hourly rate** — The wages-with-taxes-withheld portion of your pay. The floor of your package and the basis for overtime calculations.

**Travel days** — Paid (or unpaid — ask!) days allocated for getting to and from the assignment.

## V

**Vendor Management System (VMS)** — The software portal where agencies submit bids and compliance docs for hospital jobs, often run alongside an MSP.

## W

**W2 traveler** — You are the agency's employee: taxes withheld, overtime rules generally standard, allowances paid alongside wages.

**1099 traveler** — You are contracted as an independent business. Full control, full bookkeeping, fewer default protections — and a tax pro before signing, not after.
