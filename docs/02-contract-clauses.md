# Chapter 2 — Contract Clauses in Plain English

*A travel contract is a legally binding document. This chapter is education only — it explains what clauses generally mean so you can spot them; it is never legal advice. Bring anything unclear to a licensed professional or the agency itself, in writing.*

The good news: travel contracts are shorter and more repetitive than you fear. They recycle the same handful of clauses. Here they are, translated.

## The cast of characters

- **Hospital (the facility):** sets the unit, the schedule, and the rules you will work under.
- **Agency:** your employer (in a W2 setup) and the party you actually sign with.
- **You:** the traveler. Everything below ultimately lands on you.

## Guaranteed hours ("guarantee")

The phrase *"40-hour guarantee"* means: the agency ensures you are paid for 40 hours per week **if you follow their scheduling rules**. The catch lives in two places:

1. **Call-out rules.** If *you* call off, the guarantee may be reduced by the whole shift, not just the hours missed. Some contracts subtract an entire week.
2. **Low-census pools.** If the *hospital* sends you home, guarantee hours should still pay — make sure the contract says so, in writing, explicitly.

**Plain-English test:** ask the recruiter, "If the hospital cancels my shift, do the hours still pay? Show me the sentence." If the answer is a shrug, treat every number as *possible*, not *guaranteed*.

## Cancellations (the one clause that surprises new travelers)

There are two flavors:

- **Agency-side cancellation:** the agency ends your assignment early. Look for a **notice period** (often 14 days but ask) and whether you are owed compensation for prepaid housing or travel.
- **Hospital-side cancellation:** the hospital cancels an assignment through no fault of yours. Contracts commonly pay a smaller amount or nothing for the unworked remainder. Know the number *before* you sign, because a week-3 cancellation changes your monthly math badly.

Also ask, in plain words: **"Can either side cancel with zero notice?"** Some hospital-level consortium rules do exactly that. It is negotiable in some situations and not in others — but it should never be a surprise.

## Housing stipends and provided housing

Two separate choices, usually:

- **Agency-provided furnished housing:** agency owns the lease; you get keys. Pros: broken furnace is not your problem. Cons: no pets sometimes, no choosing the location.
- **Stipend (allowance) plus living on your own:** a weekly or monthly **nontaxable allowance** intended to cover lodging (and often a meals-and-incidentals allowance alongside it). Pros: flexibility. Cons: you carry the lease and the risk.

Key facts to nail down in writing:

1. Is the stipend **weekly**? Does it continue if you are cancelled early or on a shift?
2. Are stipends paid while on PTO or missing a week?
3. For provided housing: what happens if the placement ends early — any clawback obligations on you?

## Overtime and premiums

- **OT usually starts after 40 hours in a week** for W2 travelers — not after 12 hours of a shift, and not on your first paid hour of a "mandatory double."
- **Holiday pay** and **on-call rates** are entirely custom per contract. Get the dollar figure, not "premium rates apply."
- **Shift differentials** (nights, weekends) should be listed with their multiplier in the contract itself.

## The other clauses worth spotting

| Clause | What it means | Your question |
|---|---|---|
| **Completion bonus** | Paid only after finishing the full assignment | Is it prorated if the contract extends? |
| **Extension option** | Automated 4–13 week extension | Can either side decline, and by when? |
| **Non-compete / distance clause** | Facility may block a direct hire nearby | Is there a time limit? A distance cap? |
| **Float clause** | You can be sent to other units | Which units, what competencies, and can you decline? |
| **Attendance/call-out policy** | How call-offs affect pay + guarantee | What's the penalty ladder? |
| **Termination for cause** | Felony, license issues, etc. | What due process exists on your side? |
| **Drug screening & background** | Standard, benign | Which panel, and who pays? |
| **Reimbursements** | Licenses, travel, scrubs | Receipt rules and deadlines? |

## Real numbers to write down before signing anything

Make a mental (or paper) note of:

1. The **stipend per week** and how many weeks it applies to.
2. The **guaranteed hours per week** and cancellation penalty schedule.
3. The **completion bonus** and its conditions.
4. The **cancellation notice period**, for both sides.
5. **OT, holiday, on-call rates** — actual dollar figures.

If a recruiter can quote all five without checking with the hospital marketing team, that is usually a good sign the contract is clean.

## Chapter 2 takeaways

- Every number is either *guaranteed* or *possible* — ask which, and ask for the sentence that says so.
- Cancellation rules are the highest-stakes fine print. Read them first.
- Stipends are a convenience tool, not salary: ask what happens to them when things go sideways.
- You are not being difficult by asking for numbers in writing. You are doing the job of protecting your own income.
