# The Travel Nurse First-Contract Guide

*A plain-English companion for nurses signing their first travel assignment*

---

## Who this guide is for

You finished orientation years ago (or maybe just last spring), you know your way around a code, and now something has been tugging at you: the open road. Higher pay. New cities. A schedule with a built-in end date. Travel nursing is a real, achievable way to grow your career and your savings — but the first contract is where most of us get wobbly, because nobody hands nurses a manual for reading a pay package or a clause sheet.

So here is that manual.

If you have ever stared at a spreadsheet of numbers your recruiter sent and thought, *"Which of these numbers actually matter?"* — welcome. This guide walks you through it like a friend would: step by step, in plain English, never condescending.

## What this guide is (and is not)

This is an **education-only** guide. It is **never** contract advice, legal advice, tax advice, or medical advice. Every state, agency, and situation is different. When a decision actually matters — and the first contract definitely matters — put a licensed professional (an attorney, a tax preparer who works with travelers, your licensing board) on the phone. Think of this guide as the prep work that makes those conversations fast and useful.

With that said, inside you will find:

- **Agency types, decoded.** National agencies, local agencies, MSPs, vendor management systems, W2 vs 1099 — who all these players actually are.
- **Contract clauses in plain English.** Cancellations, housing stipends, overtime, guaranteed hours, completion bonuses — what the words really mean.
- **NLC compact licensing basics.** How the Nurse Licensure Compact works and how to check where you can work without new paperwork.
- **Comparing pay packages honestly.** A way to turn three different-looking offers into three actually comparable numbers.
- **Furnished-housing inspections.** How to walk into a strange apartment and know within ten minutes whether it works for you.
- **Packing and first-week routines.** What goes in the car, and how to land soft in a new city.
- **Tax-record habits.** Simple routines that keep your records tidy so a tax pro can actually help you.
- **Appendices worth printing:** a negotiation-question list, a packing checklist you can copy, and a glossary of the odd words recruiters use.

## How to use this guide

Read it in order the first time — each chapter builds on the last. After that, treat it like a reference: the appendices are designed to be printed or copied into your own notes before you talk to a recruiter.

A practical tip: keep a single notes document per contract you are considering. Every chapter here suggests things to write down, and one lived-in document beats five scattered ones.

## A note on the tone

You will not find hype here, and you will not find doom either. Travel nursing is a wonderful path for a lot of nurses and a bad fit for some, and the honest difference is usually preparation. That is the whole point of this guide: walk you past the preparation so the adventure part can be the fun part.
