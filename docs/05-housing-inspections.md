# Chapter 5 — Furnished-Housing Inspections

Thirteen weeks is a long time in a strange apartment with a mystery couch. A ten-minute, systematic walk-through — done *before* you unpack — prevents most traveler-horror stories. Whether the agency provides the unit or you lease it yourself, the same inspection habit protects you.

## Before you even tour

- **Ask the address** ahead of time and look at the commute during the hours you will actually drive. "Twelve minutes" and "twelve minutes at 6:45 a.m. shift change" are different animals.
- **Confirm the lease setup:** who holds the lease, who to call when something breaks, and what happens if the assignment ends early. These answers should be verbal *and* written.
- **Check for utilities/billing surprises:** internet speed, parking availability, laundry situation.

## The ten-minute walk-through (do it with your phone)

**Safety and structure first:**

1. **Smoke/carbon-monoxide detectors** present — press the test button (yes, really).
2. **Doors and windows** lock and latch; ground-floor windows have working locks.
3. **Water:** run hot water in the kitchen and both bathrooms; check drains and water pressure; look under sinks for moisture.
4. **Electrical:** every outlet works (bring one phone charger as a tester). We know that sounds paranoid; it takes ninety seconds.
5. **HVAC:** run the heat, then the AC, each for a few minutes.

**Then livability:**

6. **Bed frame** assembled and not broken — travelers have all been burned by the frame that folds at 3 a.m.
7. **Mattress** — check for stains, sagging, bedbugs (look at seams). Do not be polite about this.
8. **Kitchen:** fridge clean and cold, stove works, one pot, one pan, one frying pan minimum.
9. **Laundry** — machine present? On site? Quarter-operated gadget from 1994?
10. **Noise test:** stand still with the windows closed for a full minute. Street, upstairs neighbors, the HVAC unit over your bed.

## Photograph everything, day one

This matters more than any tip in this chapter:

- **Take time-stamped photos** of every room plus any pre-existing damage: wall scuffs, broken blinds, stained carpet. 
- If the unit came through the agency, **email the photos to your recruiter** with a one-line note: "Condition at move-in, [date]." If something is blamed on you at checkout, this email ends the conversation.
- If you leased it yourself, photo-document *the same day* and send to the landlord — with the same sentence.

## The "checklist before move-in" habit

Copy the packing-checklist mentality from **Appendix B** into a housing-inspection list on your phone. Same seven items every time: detectors, locks, hot water, bed, kitchen, laundry, noise. On the eleventh assignment, you will run through it without thinking, which is exactly how it should work.

## When something is wrong

- **Report in writing, immediately.** "The bedroom window does not lock" at noon is a maintenance ticket; "the bedroom window does not lock" at week nine becomes an awkward conversation.
- **For agency housing:** the agency is your contact, not the unit's property manager. Report through them and keep your thread saved.
- **For your own lease:** know the proper maintenance-reporting channel *before* you need it at 11 p.m.

## Red flags in furnished units

- The landlord or agency cannot produce basics like a working smoke detector or a lease.
- A KEYS-in-a-lockbox arrangement with no named human to contact.
- Bedrooms without an emergency exit route, or windows painted shut in bedrooms.
- A unit that needs fixtures/tasks "fixed before your arrival"… still broken at move-in, scheduled vaguely.

## Chapter 5 takeaways

- The ten-minute inspection is the cheapest insurance in travel nursing.
- **Photos + an email on day one** settle later disputes without argument.
- Safety checks (detectors, locks, exits) come before comfort checks, every time.
- Report problems in writing the same day you find them.
